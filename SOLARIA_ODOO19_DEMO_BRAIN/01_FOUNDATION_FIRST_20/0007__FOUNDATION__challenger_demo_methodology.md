# Challenger Demo Methodology (Teach, Tailor, Take Control)

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Foundation file | 0007 of 20 |
| Purpose | Bring the client an insight they did not have, tailored to their world, and convert it into momentum. Insight over argument, always. |

## 1. The stance
A Challenger demo does not ask "what do you want to see?" and play it back. It teaches the client something true and useful about their own business, tailors it to the people in the room, and takes control of the next step. It is respectful and evidence-aware: we challenge assumptions and status-quo costs, never people or past decisions. If a "challenge" would embarrass an attendee, it becomes a private conversation, not a demo moment.

## 2. Teach: the insight
For each key client challenge, produce this structure:
1. **Conventional client view:** what they currently believe ("our quoting is slow because sales needs more people").
2. **Hidden cost or overlooked risk:** what that belief is costing them ("every day of quote delay drops win rate; re-typing between CRM and Excel creates the errors that legal later fights about").
3. **Useful business insight:** the reframe, stated as something they can verify ("your problem is handoffs, not headcount: the quote passes through four tools before the customer sees it").
4. **Reframed problem:** the problem worth solving ("eliminate the handoffs and the approval wait, not add capacity").

Insight quality bar: specific to this client's process, verifiable from their own numbers, and new to at least the decision maker. If it would be true for any company, it is not an insight yet.

## 3. Tailor: to audience, industry, country and stage
- **Audience:** the same insight is framed as money for the CFO, throughput for the COO, control for the CIO, team relief for the operational manager (persona packs).
- **Industry:** use the industry pack's vocabulary, KPIs and typical process names; a machine builder hears "order-to-installation", a services firm hears "sold hours to invoiced hours".
- **Country:** statutory, language and works-council sensitivities change what is shown and how it is labelled (localisation validation always flagged).
- **Sales stage:** early stage teaches broadly and provokes curiosity; late stage teaches narrowly and de-risks the decision.

## 4. Take Control: the conversion
Every insight ends in a controlled next step, not a shrug:
- **Relevant Odoo story:** the honest capability mapping (edition + ladder labels) that answers the reframed problem.
- **Live demonstration moment:** where the storyline makes the insight visible (the moment the room leans forward).
- **Advancing question:** a question that moves the sale, asked after the moment. Patterns: "If quotes went out the same day, what would that change for your Q4 target?" or "Who in your team should stress-test this flow on your own data in a POC?"

## 5. The Challenger sheet (output form)
One row per key challenge: conventional view | hidden cost | insight | reframe | Odoo story (with labels) | demo moment | advancing question. Produced at workflow stage 9; feeds the storyline and the objection sheet.

## 6. Tone rules (non-negotiable)
- Never aggressive, never smug, never "gotcha". The client should feel smarter after the insight, not caught out.
- Never fabricate the hidden cost: derive it from their intake numbers or mark it as an estimate to validate together.
- Never use an insight to disparage the incumbent system or partner by name; critique the pattern, not the vendor.
- If the client rejects the reframe, explore it with questions; do not repeat it louder. The purpose is insight, not winning an argument.
