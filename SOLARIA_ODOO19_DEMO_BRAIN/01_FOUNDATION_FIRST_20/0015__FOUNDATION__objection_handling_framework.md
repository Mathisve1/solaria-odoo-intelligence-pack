# Objection Handling Framework

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Foundation file | 0015 of 20 |
| Purpose | The permanent method for preparing and answering objections. The deep per-objection library lives in the 4000 range; this file fixes the structure and the categories. |

## 1. The structure (produce this for every prepared objection)
1. **Objection:** as the client would actually say it.
2. **Likely underlying concern:** what is really being protected (budget, career risk, past scar tissue, control).
3. **What NOT to say:** the tempting answer that damages trust (overpromise, competitor bashing, "that never happens").
4. **Recommended response:** honest, concern-first, evidence-aware; two to four sentences a presenter can actually say.
5. **Proof or evidence needed:** what backs the response (pack reference, demo moment, reference client, validation plan).
6. **Relevant Odoo demo action:** if a live moment can answer it, name the beat.
7. **Follow-up question:** turn the objection into discovery ("what would you need to see to be comfortable on this point?").
8. **Escalation or validation requirement:** when the honest answer is "we will validate and come back", with owner and route.

## 2. The categories (the library covers all of these)
- **Cost** (licence, implementation, TCO)
- **Implementation risk** (duration, disruption, failed-project history)
- **Scalability** (users, volumes, entities, countries)
- **Security** (access, data protection, hosting)
- **Open source** ("is it serious/safe?")
- **Integrations** (the systems that stay)
- **Migration** (data, open items, history)
- **Customisation** ("we are special" / "we fear custom debt")
- **Reporting** (statutory and management)
- **Usability** (adoption, training)
- **AI** (hype scepticism and risk fear, both directions)
- **Support** (who helps us after go-live)
- **Partner dependency** ("locked into Deloitte?")
- **Community versus Enterprise** (edition and licensing confusion)

## 3. Rules of engagement
- Address the concern, not just the sentence: answering the stated words while missing the underlying fear loses the room silently.
- Honesty beats deflection: conceding a real limitation with a plan ("true today, here is how we handle it") builds more trust than a clever dodge.
- Never fabricate competitor weaknesses or Odoo strengths; competitor references stay high-level and validation-aware.
- One objection, one owner in the room: decide during preparation who answers what category (sales lead, architect, delivery lead).
- The top five prepared objections always include: the decision maker's likely objection (intake D), one edition/licensing objection when Enterprise scope appears, and one AI objection when AI is anywhere near scope.
- After the room: every objection actually raised enters the follow-up register with the response given and any validation promised.

## 4. The three-step live pattern (for unprepared objections)
1. Acknowledge and locate the concern ("fair point; that is really about migration risk, correct?").
2. Give the honest one-liner now (with the right claim label; never invent).
3. Commit to the specific follow-up ("we will validate X and include it in the summary by Friday"). Then actually route it (workflow stage 14).
