# Remote, In-Person and Multi-Presenter Demos

Extends foundations 0014, 0016, 0017. Category: DEMO_METHOD.

## Remote demos
- Camera on, gallery visible on a second screen: you must see the room you cannot feel.
- Share the window, not the desktop; notifications killed; zoom to 125 to 150 percent.
- Interaction every 5 to 7 minutes (a question, a name, a 'does this match your process?'): remote attention decays faster.
- Tech fallback: second presenter ready to share within 15 seconds; phone hotspot on the table; the recording of the rehearsal as last resort.
- Shorter is better remotely: take the next format down from what you would do in person (0014).

## In-person demos
- Arrive early enough to test the actual projector/screen with the actual laptop; HDMI is a demo risk category of its own.
- Readability from the last row beats aesthetics: test zoom from the back of the room.
- Use the physical room: hand the phone with the FSM/barcode flow to the sceptic; a device in the hand converts better than a screen at a distance.
- Watch the decision maker's attention, not the screen; the second presenter reads the room while the first drives.

## Multi-presenter facilitation
- Maximum three presenters under 60 minutes (0014); each beat has exactly one owner.
- Handovers are scripted one-liners: 'Maria takes over exactly where the order enters her warehouse.' Rehearse the handovers as their own beats.
- One person owns the clock and the parking log, visibly.
- Disagreement protocol: never correct each other on product claims in the room; the claim owner answers, others add only when invited.

## Hybrid rooms (worst case, plan hardest)
Remote decision maker plus physical room: assign one presenter to the remote audience explicitly (their camera, their questions), or the remote person silently checks out.
