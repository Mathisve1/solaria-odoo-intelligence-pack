# Demo Storyline Framework

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Foundation file | 0009 of 20 |
| Core rule | Demos are **process-led, never app-menu-led**. Follow one business object through the company; never announce "now I will show CRM, then Sales, then Inventory". |

## 1. The 12-beat storyline (canonical structure)
1. **Opening:** who we are in one line; what the next N minutes will prove; the promise ("one order, end to end, in your world").
2. **Client context:** play back their situation in their vocabulary (from intake). Thirty seconds that buy the room's trust.
3. **Business challenge:** name the top challenge(s) from the diagnosis (file 0006). Concrete, quantified where possible.
4. **Reframing / Challenger insight:** the teach moment (file 0007). One insight, cleanly delivered.
5. **Future-state vision:** one sentence per challenge: how their day looks after. No product words yet.
6. **End-to-end Odoo process:** the spine. One object travels: for example a spare-parts order from webshop to warehouse to invoice, or a machine from quote to installation to service visit. Apps appear only as stations the object passes through.
7. **Key wow moments:** two to four, placed exactly where the diagnosed challenges live. Each wow moment answers a named pain, not a generic "look how easy".
8. **Exceptions and controls:** deliberately break something (stockout, credit block, failed check) and show the system surfacing it. Exception handling convinces operators and CFOs more than happy paths.
9. **Reporting and management view:** the flow's traces appear in live pivots / dashboards; drill from figure to document. Close the loop for decision makers.
10. **AI opportunity (only if relevant):** native, labelled honestly (edition, validation); or a clearly labelled Deloitte concept. Never blur (file 0011/0018 rules).
11. **Implementation realism:** thirty honest seconds: phasing, data, adoption, what needs validation. Realism is a differentiator, not a weakness.
12. **Summary and next step:** replay the three takeaways against the three challenges; ask the advancing question; name the concrete next action (workshop, POC, reference call).

## 2. Choosing the spine object
Pick the business object closest to the client's money and the diagnosed pain: an order (trade), a machine/project (make-to-order), a ticket-to-intervention (service), a candidate (staffing), a subscription (recurring). The storyline names the object like a character ("order 1042 from Janssens BV").

## 3. Placement rules
- Wow moments only where a diagnosed challenge lives; delete orphan wow moments.
- The politically sensitive problem (intake E) gets a deliberate placement decision: featured, gently referenced, or excluded.
- The audience map (file 0008) decides which beats expand and which compress: executive-heavy room = beats 1 to 5 and 9 to 12 grow; operational room = beats 6 to 8 grow.
- Every beat has an owner (presenter), screens, and named demo records (file 0016) before it enters the runbook.

## 4. Anti-patterns (reject these plans)
- App tour ("and this is the Inventory app").
- Feature buffet: many capabilities, no narrative, no client vocabulary.
- Happy-path-only demos: no exceptions shown, no controls shown.
- Wow moments that demo well but map to nothing the client said.
- Unlabelled Enterprise or custom moments inside a Community conversation.
- A closing without a next step.

## 5. Output form
Storyline = the 12 beats with one line each, plus the spine object, plus per-beat presenter/screens/records. It feeds the minute-by-minute runbook (file 0014 governs the time budget).
