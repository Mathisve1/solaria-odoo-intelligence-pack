# Objection Handling Library (14 categories)

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Category | OBJECTION_LIBRARY (applies the 8-part structure of foundation 0015) |
| Use | Preparation source for the per-demo objection sheet; pair with the scenario files for the big narrative objections. |

## 1. Cost

- **Likely underlying concern:** Budget accountability and fear of hidden costs; often an unlike-for-unlike comparison.
- **What not to say:** Defensive price justifications; instant discount talk; licensing numbers from memory.
- **Recommended response:** Restructure the comparison to total cost of outcome: licence, implementation, integration tax, maintenance, and the cost of what cheaper options cannot do. Map must-haves to editions so scopes compare honestly.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** Edition mapping of their requirements; implementation phasing with gates; commercial desk for real numbers.
- **Relevant demo action:** Show one Enterprise-only must-have with its label so the edition delta is concrete.
- **Follow-up question:** Which three capabilities are non-negotiable? Let us price the scope that actually contains them.
- **Escalation / validation:** All pricing to the commercial track (Requires commercial validation).

## 2. Implementation risk

- **Likely underlying concern:** A failed or painful project in their history; fear for business continuity.
- **What not to say:** 'This time is different' without mechanics; timeline bravado.
- **Recommended response:** Name the failure modes and the gates that catch them: scope discipline, custom gate, mock migrations, phased go-live, hypercare. Risk is managed by method, not optimism.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** The phase/gate roadmap; customisation registry approach; reference of a comparable delivery.
- **Relevant demo action:** Configuration-speed moment: change a stage/threshold live to show standard-first velocity.
- **Follow-up question:** What went wrong last time, concretely? Let us show which gate would have caught it.
- **Escalation / validation:** Scoping workshop before any date; phase-one definition.

## 3. Scalability

- **Likely underlying concern:** Career risk of choosing 'too small'; genuine volume questions.
- **What not to say:** Anecdotes without sources; 'plenty big enough' hand-waving.
- **Recommended response:** Make it testable: agree the volume and concurrency numbers that matter and prove them in a bounded POC on realistic data.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** POC design with agreed success criteria; reference architectures (validated before citing).
- **Relevant demo action:** Demo on realistic data volumes, never toy lists.
- **Follow-up question:** Which numbers would settle this: orders/day, users, entities? Let us write them into the POC.
- **Escalation / validation:** Performance proof through POC (Requires technical validation).

## 4. Security

- **Likely underlying concern:** Data protection, access control, hosting exposure.
- **What not to say:** Marketing-grade security claims; skipping the question to return to features.
- **Recommended response:** Walk the actual model: roles, record rules, audit trails, hosting options, patching chain. Offer the security annex session with their security team.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** Live role-based access demo; security annex agenda; hosting model documentation.
- **Relevant demo action:** Switch users live and watch the same screen change scope.
- **Follow-up question:** Which scenario worries you most: internal misuse, external breach, or vendor access? Let us walk exactly that one.
- **Escalation / validation:** Deployment-specific hardening and compliance mapping (Requires technical validation).

## 5. Open source

- **Likely underlying concern:** Equating open licence with unsupported hobby software.
- **What not to say:** Ideological open-source evangelism; dismissing the concern.
- **Recommended response:** Separate licence from accountability: vendor-backed product, subscription support, partner delivery, and inspectability as a due-diligence advantage.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** Odoo SA release/security process; Deloitte delivery accountability; the annex session.
- **Relevant demo action:** Show the audit trail and upgrade cadence facts calmly.
- **Follow-up question:** If support accountability were contractual and named, does the licence model still concern you?
- **Escalation / validation:** Support/SLA specifics to the commercial track.

## 6. Integrations

- **Likely underlying concern:** The systems that must stay; fear of interface sprawl.
- **What not to say:** 'We integrate with everything'; connector claims without checks.
- **Recommended response:** Name the boundary per system honestly: what Odoo replaces, what stays, and the contract for each interface (owner, direction, monitoring). Fewer, well-owned interfaces beat clever middleware.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** Connector existence checked in the catalog per system; integration inventory from intake F.
- **Relevant demo action:** Show a native boundary honestly: where Odoo stops and the carrier/bank/PSP begins.
- **Follow-up question:** Which systems are non-negotiable survivors? Let us draw the boundary per system next session.
- **Escalation / validation:** Per-connector verification (Requires technical validation per system).

## 7. Migration

- **Likely underlying concern:** Data loss, downtime, team bandwidth fears.
- **What not to say:** 'Migration is easy'; volume promises before assessment.
- **Recommended response:** Method over bravado: minimum viable data scope, at least two mock migrations with reconciliation gates, archives kept queryable instead of migrated.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** Mock-migration plan; reconciliation report samples; data-quality sampling first.
- **Relevant demo action:** Demo data that looks migrated: their vocabulary, opening balances, open orders.
- **Follow-up question:** Which data must be live on day one, and which merely findable? That line defines the risk.
- **Escalation / validation:** Migration assessment before commitments (Requires technical validation).

## 8. Customisation

- **Likely underlying concern:** Upgrade debt fear or its opposite: fear that standard will not fit.
- **What not to say:** 'You will never need custom' or 'we can build anything' (both wrong).
- **Recommended response:** The ladder with governance: standard first, configuration, Studio (E, governed), automation, and a custom gate that requires justification, an owner and registry entry. Discipline is contractual, not aspirational.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** The standard-before-custom framework; customisation registry approach; a walked example.
- **Relevant demo action:** Resolve one of their 'needs custom' items live via configuration or Studio (labelled).
- **Follow-up question:** Bring your three scariest requirements to a ladder workshop; let us see where they honestly land.
- **Escalation / validation:** Ladder workshop on their requirements.

## 9. Reporting

- **Likely underlying concern:** Statutory anxiety and management-reporting fatigue.
- **What not to say:** Statutory promises without country checks; BI-replacement claims.
- **Recommended response:** Split the topic in three: operational (live pivots, Community), management (spreadsheet dashboards, Enterprise, labelled), statutory (engine plus country packs, expert-validated always). Different answers, honestly given.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** Country pack existence per their countries (catalog check); the drill-down demo; BI boundary statement.
- **Relevant demo action:** Drill from a statement figure to the source document (E, labelled).
- **Follow-up question:** Which ten reports run this business? With samples, we map each to its honest layer.
- **Escalation / validation:** Statutory completeness per country (Requires localisation validation).

## 10. Usability / adoption

- **Likely underlying concern:** Fear their teams will reject another system.
- **What not to say:** 'Everyone loves Odoo'; dismissing adoption as training's problem.
- **Recommended response:** Adoption is designed: fewer clicks on the frequent tasks (counted, shown), role-scoped screens, and a pilot group with real users before rollout. The demo counts clicks on purpose.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** The timed before/after on their most frequent task; pilot design.
- **Relevant demo action:** The end-user beat: their task, half the clicks, timed live.
- **Follow-up question:** Which task, if visibly easier, would win your sceptics over? We will demo exactly that one.
- **Escalation / validation:** Pilot with named users as part of phase one.

## 11. AI

- **Likely underlying concern:** Both hype-scepticism and risk-fear, sometimes in the same room.
- **What not to say:** Accuracy numbers; autonomy promises; concept-product blur; dismissing either fear.
- **Recommended response:** The four-way separation said plainly: native capabilities (E, gated), Deloitte concepts (labelled), custom opportunities (governed), and validation always. The validation queue and the AI register are the answer to both audiences.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** The AI governance one-pager; same-day rehearsed OCR moment; the honesty beat on what does not exist.
- **Relevant demo action:** One bill through the validation queue; the human check as the hero.
- **Follow-up question:** Would a bounded, governed pilot on your own documents settle both the value and the risk question?
- **Escalation / validation:** Risk/legal review; pilot with metrics (Requires technical validation).

## 12. Support

- **Likely underlying concern:** Who helps after go-live; abandonment fear.
- **What not to say:** Vague 'we are always there'; support promises sales cannot bind.
- **Recommended response:** Name the chain: Odoo SA product support per subscription, Deloitte delivery and care model, the operations handbook, and the governance rhythm. Roles on paper, not vibes.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** The support/RACI one-pager; hypercare model from the roadmap method.
- **Relevant demo action:** Show the operations surfaces honestly (admin, logs, registry).
- **Follow-up question:** What did good support look like in your best vendor relationship? We will map our model to that.
- **Escalation / validation:** Support scope/SLA to the commercial track.

## 13. Partner dependency

- **Likely underlying concern:** Lock-in fear: 'are we marrying Deloitte?'
- **What not to say:** Dismissing it; vague 'the code is open' as a complete answer.
- **Recommended response:** Reduce the exit cost honestly: documented configuration workbook, registry of every customisation, standard-first design that any Odoo partner can read, and knowledge transfer as a deliverable.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** The documentation deliverables list; the standard-first design principle; the partner ecosystem's existence.
- **Relevant demo action:** Show the configuration workbook concept and the registry as artifacts.
- **Follow-up question:** Which artifacts in your hands would make you feel free to leave, and therefore safe to stay?
- **Escalation / validation:** Deliverables list into the proposal.

## 14. Community vs Enterprise

- **Likely underlying concern:** Confusion or suspicion about editions and what is being sold.
- **What not to say:** Edition laundering; licensing guesses; 'it is all just Odoo'.
- **Recommended response:** The clean explanation: Community is the base layer (named capabilities), Enterprise adds named layers (Studio, accounting depth, the service apps, AI); every demo moment we show is labelled; subscription contents are confirmed commercially, in writing.
- **Proof / evidence needed:** The edition map; labelled storyline; the commercial validation promise.
- **Relevant demo action:** Point at an (E) label in the demo and explain the discipline itself: it builds trust.
- **Follow-up question:** Shall we mark your must-have list with editions together, right now?
- **Escalation / validation:** Subscription/packaging specifics (Requires commercial validation, always).
