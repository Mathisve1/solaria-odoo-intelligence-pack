# Challenger Scenario: AI is too risky

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Category | CHALLENGER scenario (applies foundations 0007 and 0015) |
| Tone | Insight over argument; respectful always; competitor references high-level and validation-aware |

## Situation
Compliance/works-council/security stakeholders fear AI in core processes.

## Underlying concern
Data leakage, wrong outputs acted upon, regulatory exposure, workforce anxiety.

## Challenger insight
AI risk in an ERP is a design property: human-in-the-loop queues, deterministic domains excluded (posting, tax), access-leakage testing and an AI register make AI auditable. The risky version is shadow AI: employees pasting company data into public chatbots because the governed path does not exist.

## Reframe
The choice is not AI versus no AI; it is governed AI versus shadow AI.

## Recommended response pattern
- Lead with the governance framework, not the features
- show the validation queue as the control it is
- name the EU AI Act posture for high-risk domains (recruitment: human decides, always)

## Demo action
The OCR flow with the human check as the hero; the governance one-pager on the table

## Advancing question
Would a governance-first pilot (register, human sign-off, leakage tests) let your compliance team say yes to a bounded scope?

## Never say
'The AI is always right'; autonomy promises; dismissing works-council concerns

## Validation needed
Risk/legal review; leakage tests; pilot metrics before any scale-up
