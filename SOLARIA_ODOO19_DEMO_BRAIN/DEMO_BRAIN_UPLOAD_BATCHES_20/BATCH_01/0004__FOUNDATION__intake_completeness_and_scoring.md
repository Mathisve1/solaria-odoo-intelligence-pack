# Intake Completeness and Scoring

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Foundation file | 0004 of 20 |
| Purpose | One number that disciplines the whole brain: how much do we actually know about this client? |

## 1. The score (0 to 100)
Compute by summing the weights of answered intake areas. "Answered" means specific to this client; industry defaults and guesses do not count.

| Intake area | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Industry (+ sub-industry) | 10 | Minimum field |
| Meeting type / sales stage | 10 | Minimum field |
| Duration and format | 5 | Minimum field |
| Audience (roles + at least one objective/concern per key attendee) | 15 | Minimum field |
| Core business challenges (at least the top 2, with current process) | 20 | Minimum field, heaviest weight |
| Demo objectives (understand / believe / feel / next action) | 10 | Minimum field |
| Relevant scope (in and out) | 10 | Minimum field |
| Client profile depth (size, entities, countries, current ERP) | 5 | |
| Opportunity context (competitors, criteria, decision date) | 5 | |
| Constraints and risks (section H) | 5 | |
| Data/localisation/integration specifics (sections F detail) | 5 | |

## 2. Bands and permitted output

| Score | Band | Permitted output |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 39 | **Insufficient** | No demo plan. Restate what is known, ask the highest-impact questions (max 5 at a time), offer the quick intake. |
| 40 to 59 | **Provisional** | Outline only: candidate storyline, candidate scope, candidate wow moments. Every gap becomes a labelled assumption. Open with the score and the three most dangerous unknowns. |
| 60 to 79 | **Workable** | A useful demo plan following the output contract, with a visible risk section and assumptions distinguished from client facts. |
| 80 to 100 | **Client-specific** | The full detailed strategy: complete output contract, minute-by-minute flow, objection preparation, data list, rehearsal checklist. |

## 3. Minimum required information (hard gate)
Regardless of the numeric score, a full plan is blocked until all seven are known: **industry; meeting type; duration; audience; core challenges; demo objective; relevant scope.** If six of seven are present, say exactly which one blocks and why it matters.

## 4. Prioritising missing information
When asking questions, order by expected impact on the demo design:
1. Core business challenges (they define the storyline).
2. Audience (it defines emphasis, language and wow moments).
3. Demo objectives (they define success).
4. Meeting type and duration (they define scope limits, file 0014).
5. Industry specifics (they select the industry pack defaults).
6. Scope boundaries (they prevent overreach).
7. Everything else.

## 5. Honesty rules around the score
- Always show the score and band when producing any demo output.
- Never inflate the score by counting defaults or assumptions as answers.
- If the user insists on a full plan below the gate, produce the provisional outline instead and say plainly why the full plan would be falsely precise.
- Recompute and mention the score whenever new intake information arrives.
