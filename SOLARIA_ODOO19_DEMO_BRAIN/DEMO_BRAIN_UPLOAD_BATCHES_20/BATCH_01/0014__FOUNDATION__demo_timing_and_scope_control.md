# Demo Timing and Scope Control

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Foundation file | 0014 of 20 |
| Purpose | Overloaded demos fail. These limits are hard defaults; exceeding them requires an explicit, stated reason in the plan. |

## 1. Format limits (hard defaults)

| Format | Main processes (max) | Wow moments (max) | Depth | Q&A budget | Exclude |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15-min teaser | 1 | 1 | Surface: one flow, one insight | 3 min | Configuration screens, exceptions, reporting depth, AI unless it IS the topic |
| 30-min executive demo | 1 | 2 | One spine flow + management view | 7 min | Settings, field-level detail, technical annexes |
| 45-min standard demo | 2 | 3 | Spine flow + one exception + reporting | 10 min | Second industry variants, admin surfaces |
| 60-min functional demo | 2 to 3 | 3 | Full 12-beat storyline, one deep exception chapter | 12 min | Technical architecture (park to annex) |
| 90-min deep-dive | 3 | 4 | Storyline + one domain deep chapter + technical annex slot | 20 min | Everything not tied to a diagnosed challenge |
| Half-day workshop | 4 to 5 (as chapters) | 5 | Chaptered storylines per audience group, hands-on moments | Built into each chapter | Uninterrupted monologue beyond 20 min |
| Proof of concept | Per POC scope doc | n/a | Client data, agreed success criteria (09 range files) | n/a | Anything outside the signed POC scope |

## 2. Allocation rules
- Reserve the Q&A budget explicitly in the runbook; a demo that consumes its Q&A time has failed its planning, not succeeded in content.
- The opening (beats 1 to 5 of the storyline) never exceeds 20% of total time; the client came to see the thing work.
- Every wow moment gets breathing room: at least one minute of silence-tolerant pacing after it lands; do not sprint past your best moment.
- Transitions cost time: each additional "main process" costs about 3 minutes of context switching; budget it.
- Multi-presenter sessions add handover minutes; more than three presenters in under 60 minutes is a planning error.

## 3. Scope-cut protocol (when the plan exceeds limits)
Cut in this order: (1) orphan wow moments (unmapped to challenges); (2) the third process; (3) reporting depth (keep one drill-down); (4) the AI beat unless AI is a stated objective; (5) exception chapter shrinks to one exception. Never cut: the client-context beat, the challenge naming, the closing next step.

## 4. Overrun recovery (in the room)
Pre-mark two "accordion" beats in the runbook that can compress from minutes to one sentence each. If overrun hits, compress accordions, protect the closing: takeaways plus next step ALWAYS happen, even in ninety seconds. Park new requests with the parking phrase: "logged; we will show that properly in the follow-up rather than rush it now."

## 5. The overload test
If the plan needs the phrase "and then quickly show", it is overloaded. Fewer things, landed properly, win demos. The brain applies these limits at workflow stage 8 and rejects storylines that violate them, proposing the cut list instead.
